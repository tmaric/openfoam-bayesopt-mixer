Part one

The problem

What the device is, which numbers we are allowed to change, and what exactly counts as “better”.

A passive micromixer: no moving parts, only geometry

A flat channel, 1 mm tall and 24 mm long. Two streams enter side by side — one with scalar T = 1, one with T = 0. They must leave mixed.

There is no stirrer. The only thing we design is the shape of the walls.

FixedValue
Channel height H1 mm
Total length L24 mm
Unit cells N5, strong side alternating
Mean velocity0.01 m/s
Fluidwater

One unit cell — and every parameter on it

Split

A center baffle of thickness ts over length Ls divides the stream in two.

Interact

Over Lc two cosine deflectors push in, aweak and astrong — unequal on purpose, and the strong one swaps wall from cell to cell.

Merge

A thinner baffle tm over Lm brings the halves back together, folded.

Drawn from the parameters by renderCell(), following the section layout in alternating_deflector_cad.py. Repeat it five times, moving the strong deflector to the other wall each time, and you have the device.

Why this is hard: laminar flow, and diffusion that will not help

Re = 10
Steady and laminar. No turbulence to do the mixing for us.
Sc = 1000
Momentum diffuses a thousand times faster than the scalar.
Pe = 104
In the residence time, diffusion crosses only a sliver of the channel.

So mixing must come from geometry. The walls have to stretch and fold the interface until diffusion has a short enough distance left to finish. Every bit of that stretching costs pressure.

Six numbers we are allowed to change

BO coordinateBoundsEffect on the device
aweak0 – 0.12 Hshallow deflector amplitude
astrong,ratio0 – 1→ astrong, the asymmetry that drives transverse flow
ts0.04 – 0.15 Hsplit-section baffle thickness
tm,ratio0 – 1→ tm, the merge-section baffle
Lc0.40 – 2.40 Hinteraction length
Ls,ratio0 – 1→ splits the rest of the cell into Ls and Lm

Three of them are ratios, not lengths. The next two slides are why.

Search raw lengths and the box contains rubbish

aweak = 0.46 astrong = 0.50 ts = 0.06 tm = 0.14

A perfectly legal corner of a box over raw lengths. The deflectors nearly touch — a 0.04 H gap, below the 0.05 H the mesh can resolve — and the merge baffle is thicker than the split baffle, so the transition steps the wrong way.

cfMesh leaves zero-area faces at features like this and simpleFoam then dies on a floating-point exception. The optimizer would spend real CFD time discovering that.

Ratios map the unit box onto only buildable shapes

aweak = 0.08 direct astrong,ratio = 0.43 → astrong = 0.22 ts = 0.10 direct tm,ratio = 0.30 → tm = 0.044

A ratio of 0.43 means “43 % of the way through the interval that is still admissible given the other parameters”. The interval endpoints move; the ratio cannot leave [0, 1].

Same unit box, three corners, three real devices

near the origin

Barely perturbed. Cheap to pump, mixes almost nothing.

mid-box

Strong asymmetry, moderate cost.

the far corner

Maximum obstruction — and still meshable.

Every corner of [0,1]6 is a geometry that can be built, meshed and solved. The optimizer never wastes a CFD run learning that.

Objective 1 — what the device costs to run

Jdp(x) = Δp(x) / Δpstraight

The pressure the pump must supply, divided by what an empty channel of the same size would need. It reads directly as “how many times more expensive than a plain pipe”.

2.874 Pa
the straight-channel reference — within 0.039 % of the analytic parallel-plate solution ρ·12νUL/H², which is how we know the CFD chain is trustworthy before optimizing anything.

Objective 2 — how much is still unmixed

Look only at the outlet. Every parcel of fluid leaving carries some concentration T. If the device worked, every parcel carries the same value — the average, 0.5.

1
Untouched. Half the outlet is pure T = 1, half pure T = 0. The spread across parcels is as large as it can possibly be.
0
Perfect. Every parcel is exactly 0.5. No spread at all.

The intensity of segregation is that spread, scaled so untouched = 1 and perfect = 0.

Two details that stop it lying to us

Jmix(x) = Is,flux = σ²T,flux / 0.25

Weighted by flow, not by area

A plain area average counts a stagnant corner as much as the fast core. Mix the corner beautifully and the number improves while the fluid that actually leaves is untouched.

Weighting each parcel by its mass flux measures the stream that really exits.

Reported as a quality, not a defect

mixing index = 1 − √Is,flux

The number rises as the device gets better, and it is what the literature quotes — the only way our result can be compared with anyone else’s.

Both objectives come from custom OpenFOAM function objects: pressureDrop and patchMixingQuality.

Forbidden: a shape that cannot be made

  • features thinner than 10 µm — below the etch resolution and below the mesh’s fine cell;
  • a merge baffle thinner than 2 fine cells;
  • deflector peaks closer than 4 cells — a crack, not a channel;
  • a split-to-merge step the mesh cannot resolve.

Rejected by interval arithmetic before the CAD runs. No CFD time is spent on a sliver.

Forbidden: a result that cannot be trusted

  • an inlet or outlet face not exactly on x = 0 / x = L;
  • a mesh checkMesh will not pass;
  • a flow rate off by more than 3 % from U·A;
  • a mass imbalance above 10−5;
  • an outlet metric still drifting when we stop.

Fail any one and no objective is written at all. The design is recorded as failed, not as a number.

The bar was set before any data was taken

0.60
mixing index a design must reach to be called useful
20 Pa
the pressure budget: the hypervolume reference point and the publication target. The automatic screening gate checks the mixing index and a failure fraction ≤ 25 %.

Declaring the gate first is what makes a later “no” a result instead of a rationalization.

Part two

Bayesian optimization

From Bayes’ theorem, to a belief about a whole function, to knowing which simulation to run next.

John is quiet, nerdy, and loves solving logic puzzles.

Is John more likely to be a mathematician or a librarian?

Librarian — although the description fits a mathematician

The description does fit a mathematician better. Say eight times better.

But librarians outnumber mathematicians by roughly 20 to 1.

20 ÷ 8 = 2.5. Librarian wins two and a half to one: P(mathematician | description) ≈ 0.29.

P(H | D) = P(D | H) · P(H) / P(D)

  • P(H) — the prior: how common before we look;
  • P(D|H) — the likelihood: how well H explains what we saw;
  • P(H|D) — the posterior: what to believe now.

The intuition that fails here is the one that fails in optimization: we judge by fit to the evidence and forget how much space the hypothesis occupied to begin with.

Bayes’ theorem is an area ratio

The whole square is 1. The mathematician column is 1/21 wide; each dashed line marks how deep D cuts into its column. The posterior is the blue share of what the two cuts leave. Same numbers as the theory deck: prior odds 1:20, likelihood ratio 8:1, posterior odds 1:2.5.

Our unknown is not a number — it is a function

x ↦ J(x)

Six geometry numbers in, one objective out. We have no formula for it, and every single evaluation costs a CFD run.

So we need a prior over functions, not over numbers. That is what a Gaussian process is.

A GP is one assumption: similar designs behave similarly

The GP does not know the function. It assumes only that if two designs are close, their objectives are close — and it makes “close” precise with a kernel.

Feed it the runs you have; Bayes’ rule turns the prior into a posterior. Because everything is Gaussian, that posterior is closed form.

Matérn-5/2 with ARD — automatic relevance determination: one length scale per parameter. The model learns, on its own, which of the six actually matter — and tells you.

A long length scale means that parameter barely moves the objective — a free sensitivity analysis, if a rough one with few runs. You will use it in Task 5.

What you get back is a mean and an error bar, everywhere

Computed live: squared-exponential kernel, exact posterior. The mean interpolates the five points and the variance collapses there because the algebra is real.

Both come from one factorization and a few products

μ(x) = m + k(x, X)T (K + σn²I)−1 (y − m)

σ²(x) = k(x, x) − k(x, X)T (K + σn²I)−1 k(x, X)

X — the n designs simulated so far; y their results.

K = k(X, X) — their n×n covariance; k(x, X) — the vector of covariances between a new design and each of them.

k(x, x) — the prior variance at x, before any data.

m — the constant prior mean BoTorch learns; σn² — the noise.

n is at most 112 in this campaign. One Cholesky factorization of K, then each candidate costs a few matrix-vector products: a million candidates in under a second, against minutes per CFD run here and hundreds of core-hours for a realistic 3-D case.

The answer to "should it not be k(x, x)?": k(x, x) is the first term of the variance. In the mean and in the correction term the object is the vector k(x, X), one entry per simulated design — writing k(x, x) there would be a scalar and dimensionally wrong. sigma^2 here is the variance of the latent f; a new observation adds sigma_n^2.

σ is not decoration. It is the model telling you where it is guessing — which is exactly where the next expensive run is worth buying.

Combining them: one number, two instincts

αUCB(x) = μ(x) + κ · σ(x)

μ — exploit

Go where the model already predicts a good answer. Alone, it sticks in the first decent basin it finds.

σ — explore

Go where the model is ignorant. Alone, it samples the emptiest corners forever and refines nothing.

κ — the dial

How many error bars of optimism you allow. The next three slides turn it.

A test function: the truth we are not allowed to see

The Forrester function is the standard 1-D benchmark for exactly this — one sharp global optimum and a deceptive local one.

f(x) = −(6x−2)² sin(12x−4)

We are allowed five evaluations. The dashed gray curve is the truth; the optimizer never sees it.

Green tick: the real optimum, x = 0.757.

Shown as a maximization to match the UCB story; our real objectives are minimized, where the identical argument runs with mu minus kappa sigma.

Optimizing the acquisition is a different kind of problem

1 · FitMaximize the marginal likelihood for the kernel hyperparameters.
→
2 · Search αMulti-start L-BFGS, 1024 raw samples, 32 restarts.
→
3 · SimulateOne CAD → mesh → flow → scalar chain.
→

Step 2 is a global search and it is allowed to be greedy — restart everywhere, evaluate a million times — because its objective is two matrix products, not a solver.

The expensive function is called exactly once per outer iteration.

Data efficiency is why this fits CFD at all

MethodEvaluations, 6-D
Full factorial, 5 levels15 625
Genetic algorithm10³ – 104
Adjoint (where available)10¹ – 10², local
Bayesian optimization10¹ – 10², global

This 2-D case runs in minutes, so a factorial would merely be wasteful. Scale to a 3-D device at 10²–10³ core-hours per design and the difference stops being about money: 15 625 runs is not a long campaign, it is an impossible one.

A hundred well-chosen runs is a project. Ten thousand is a proposal that gets rejected.

Two objectives: a front, not a winner

With one objective there is a best design. With two there is a set — every design where mixing cannot improve without paying more pressure.

Both axes are minimized, so better is down and to the left. A gray point is dominated: some orange point beats it on BOTH objectives at once.

Turning a whole front into one number

To say one front is better than another, you need a single number. The standard one is the hypervolume: the area of objective space the front dominates, measured against a fixed reference point.

Push the front down and left and the shaded staircase grows. That growth is the only thing the optimizer is trying to maximize.

The reference point is a real modeling choice. It says how bad a design has to be before it stops counting at all. Ours is declared in config as (20 Pa, Is = 1) — move it and you silently change what “improvement” means.

Blue: what the current front already dominates. Orange: the extra area one candidate design would add — its hypervolume improvement.

So the acquisition asks: which design adds the most area?

qLogNEHVI — q-batch Log Noisy Expected Hypervolume Improvement

Expected

The GP gives a distribution over where a candidate will land, so the added area is an expectation, not a guess.

Noisy

Our observations are not exact: every design gets a new mesh, and every solve stops at a tolerance. Discretization error itself is a deterministic bias, not noise.

Log

The raw improvement underflows to zero over most of the box. The log form keeps the optimizer able to see a gradient.

No weighted sum, and no penalty for failures

No scalarizing into a weighted sum — that would need weights nobody can justify, and it would quietly hide the trade-off you are trying to expose. Failed designs are excluded, never given a fictitious penalty.

Part three

Building it

OpenFOAM, Bayesian optimization, and what actually changed now that agents write the code.

The bottleneck in computational engineering was never typing. It was trust.

An agent will write you a CAD generator, a Snakefile, a GP model and a cluster profile in an afternoon — confidently, whether or not any of it is correct.

So the scarce skill moved. It is no longer knowing the API — it is stating, in executable form, what “working” means.

Write the test that quantifies the result first

You cannot write “the mixer should mix well”. A top-level test forces you to commit to a number, a tolerance and a reference — the same three things a referee will demand.

0.039 %
the straight channel against ρ·12νUL/H². Written before the solver setup it validates.

Four levels, top down

1
Physical — the straight channel matches the analytic solution. This test is the study.
2
Structural — inlet area equals H × depth; mass imbalance below 10−5.
3
Numerical — the outlet metric is stationary, not merely residual-converged.
4
Unit — does the parameter transform respect its interval?

Most projects write only level 4 — and level 4 catches none of the failures that matter.

What went wrong here, and why no solver noticed

The CAD generator decided which faces were inlets by looking at which way they pointed — not at where they were.

Every baffle and every deflector has faces pointing along -x, exactly like the real inlet does. So all of them were labeled “inlet” too.

The consequence: OpenFOAM dutifully injected fluid and scalar through the middle of the device — out of the obstacles — instead of only at x = 0.

real inlet
1 × H
one face at x = 0
what was tagged
2.85 × H
plus every obstacle face

The mixing it measured was partly fluid being injected after the mixing section. The numbers were not noisy. They were meaningless.

The repair: classify by POSITION, not orientation, and verify it by rebuilding the patch polygons from constant/polyMesh independently of the CAD that produced them.

Nothing crashed. Every solver converged. checkMesh said “Mesh OK”. The Pareto fronts looked plausible.

28
designs discarded — an entire campaign, invalidated by a bug that no exit code, no residual and no mesh check could ever have caught.

Only a test that knew where an inlet is supposed to be could find this. That is what a top-level, physical test buys you, and it is why it gets written first.

Codify the tests in a language that outlives the session

Snakemake

Rules with declared inputs and outputs. The DAG is the specification.

Validator scripts

validate_*.py and verify_*_convergence.py: patch geometry, flow balance, objective stationarity.

Inline assertions

The non-negotiables: Mesh OK, bad-face count, the analytic pressure check.

The common property: a machine can re-run all of it without you, on another host, next year.

If the test lives only in a chat transcript, it does not exist

An agent cannot read it

A bespoke checking script has to be re-explained every session — time spent re-deriving what a rule name would have stated.

It will not run elsewhere

Home-grown harnesses hard-code paths and core counts. The same test must run on a laptop and on the cluster, or it proves nothing about the cluster.

An engineer cannot audit it

A reviewer knows what a Snakemake rule or a validation script does. They should not have to reverse-engineer your conventions to trust a number.

Separate the writer from the reviewer

An agent that just wrote a function is the worst possible reviewer of it — its context is full of reasons the code should work.

  • one session, one model, develops;
  • a clean session — ideally a different model — tests and reports;
  • the reviewer gets the artifacts and the acceptance criteria, never the reasoning.

The failure modes are uncorrelated. Two models rarely hallucinate the same API in the same place, and a fresh context has no sunk cost in the design.

Live example: a laptop cartesian2DMesh aborting with free(): invalid pointer. The workflow was blamed first. Running the binary outside the workflow located it in one step.

  • Provenance. Every template file is an input: change the mesh dict and everything downstream re-runs. Nothing stale survives.
  • Honest failure. A rule either produced its output or it did not.

The cost asymmetry is the whole argument

~1 s · one core
Optimize the acquisition
~3 s · one core
One CFD run, this 2-D case
~9 min · 2 cores
One CFD run, a 3-D device

The surrogate half never needs the cluster, and it does not care which row it is steering. The driver runs on one core for the whole campaign; Snakemake sends only the simulations to the queue.

Batch BO: available, and often not the point

q > 1 proposes several designs at once and fills a machine. The acquisition already supports it.

With a budget of ~100 simulations the sequential loop is usually better, not merely simpler.

  • every design is chosen knowing all previous results;
  • a batch of 8 makes 8 decisions from stale information.

q > 1 when wall-clock binds — a deadline, a reservation, an idle machine. q = 1 when the simulation count binds, which in CFD it nearly always does.

This campaign fixes q = 1 in config and the driver refuses anything else, so the record cannot drift.

Semi-automatic, not fire-and-forget

ARD — automatic relevance determination — is a free sensitivity analysis. A huge length scale means that parameter does nothing across its whole range. You get it after every evaluation, at no cost — read it as a hint, not a verdict, while runs are few.

One study becomes the prior for the next

When a screen says the topology cannot reach the target, the right move is not more samples. It is a new hypothesis.

P(topology works | 12 designs) ≪ P(topology works)

Bayes at the level above the optimizer. The prior for the next campaign — which bounds are plausible, which mechanism is needed, what asymmetry costs in pressure — is entirely inherited from the one that failed.

A negative result with a predeclared gate is cheap, publishable and reusable. Without one it is just a shrug.

What CAD-in-the-loop BO delivers

Manufacturable by construction

Feature-size limits live in the parameterization, so every proposal is a shape that can be etched or printed.

A trade-off, not a point

The output is a front. Choosing on it is an engineering decision with a pump curve attached.

Auditable

Every design keeps its CAD parameters, mesh report, flow balance and logs.

Concretely: microreactors, lab-on-chip, process intensification — devices where geometry is the process, and a prototype costs weeks.

Task 2

Which keys actually shape the deflectors?

Open research/baselines/strong_alternating.yaml. It contains, among others:

w_s: 0.45 a_weak: 0.05 delta: 0.30 a_strong: 0.35

Set a_weak and a_strong to anything you like and the geometry does not change.

Which keys does the CAD generator actually read, and how do they produce the two amplitudes? Answer from the source, not from the names.

Answer 2

It reads ws and δ. The rest is provenance.

grep -n "a_weak\|a_strong" FlowCase/alternating_deflector_cad.py # no hits grep -nE '_p\[|_p\.get' FlowCase/alternating_deflector_cad.py # w_s, delta, k, ...

The generator computes one base intrusion and adds the bias to alternating walls:

aweak = 0.5 − ws

astrong = aweak + δ

Check: 0.5 − 0.45 = 0.05, and 0.05 + 0.30 = 0.35. Both match the file.

a_weak and a_strong are written by the BO driver so each design records the physical amplitudes it meant. They are never read back.

The habit this teaches is the one that matters: a field name is a claim, the source is the evidence.

Task 3

Predict, then measure

The strong_alternating baseline uses δ = 0.30 and reaches 11.02 Pa at a mixing index of 0.1061. The straight channel from Task 1: 1.437 Pa at 0.0681.

Halve the asymmetry to δ = 0.15 and run it.

Write your prediction down first: do pressure and mixing go up or down, and which one changes more?

Committing to a prediction before the run is the entire discipline. It is also how you notice when a result is suspicious.

Snakemake is target-driven, not a file watcher.

Deleting an intermediate nobody is currently asking for changes nothing. Ask for objectives.csv again and the chain is rebuilt only as far back as the first missing file.

What does not re-run: the CAD and the mesh. Their outputs are still on disk, so the expensive geometry and meshing are skipped entirely.

Nothing declares an order. Each rule declares what it needs and what it makes — which is why a 112-design campaign resumes cleanly after a crash.

Appendix

Reproducing this study

Every number in this deck, from a clean clone, in four steps.

Step 1 — what you need, and what you do not

You need

  • git, and at least two cores — every solve runs decomposed;
  • Apptainer — and for building the image, either the uidmap package or a setuid installation;
  • about 4 GB of disk for the image and a first campaign.

You do not need

  • OpenFOAM, cfMesh, CadQuery, PyTorch, BoTorch or Snakemake installed;
  • a cluster — a laptop runs the whole study;
  • an internet connection, once the image is built.

The image carries OpenFOAM v2512, cfMesh, CadQuery, BoTorch, Snakemake, ParaView and ffmpeg.

Step 2 — clone and build the environment

git clone https://github.com/tmaric/openfoam-bayesopt-mixer.git cd openfoam-bayesopt-mixer ./apptainer/build.sh # builds apptainer/padm.sif, or $PADM_SIF (~20 min) PADM_REMOTE_HOST=you@cluster PADM_REMOTE_DIR=/scratch/you/padm-image \ ./apptainer/build.sh --remote # or: build on a cluster, copy the .sif back

Then build the study’s OpenFOAM function objects inside the image. They are dlopened by the container’s OpenFOAM and produce both objectives — without them every design yields an empty objectives.csv.

export PADM_SIF="${PADM_SIF:-$PWD/apptainer/padm.sif}" # or your own path apptainer shell --bind "$PWD" "$PADM_SIF" Apptainer> ./Allwclean && ./Allwmake

Allwclean first is not optional when switching environments: wmake keys its object files on $WM_OPTIONS, which is linux64GccDPInt32Opt for every OpenFOAM version, so a stale tree looks current and is not.

Step 3 — run the campaign

Still inside the image, from the study directory. --campaign research selects the archived study’s settings: five cells, tight tolerances, a few minutes per design. Without it you get the workshop version from the exercises.

cd PlanarAlternatingDeflectorMixer python3 research_sequence.py status --campaign research # what stage are we at? python3 research_sequence.py next --max-new-evaluations 1 \ --profile profiles/local --campaign research

The driver is gated and advances one bounded step at a time: three matched baselines first, then the twelve-design screen, and the full campaign only if the screen passes.

On a cluster, from a host shell

export PADM_BIND=/scratch/$USER sbatch -A "$PADM_SLURM_ACCOUNT" --parsable \ --export=ALL,N=1 run-bo.sbatch >> .padm_jobs

sbatch is not in the image, and run-bo.sbatch runs the research campaign by default. See CLUSTER.md.

Rebuild the figures — of a campaign you ran

snakemake visualize \ --workflow-profile profiles/local \ --config results_dir=results/corrected_boundary_v3

Step 4 — check you got what we got

At research fidelity the straight baseline is the first thing the driver runs, and it is checkable against theory. To see the field, from the case directory inside the image: paraview FlowCase.foam.
